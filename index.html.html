Train on Earth. Settle on the Moon and Mars.

Before anyone builds a base on the Moon or Mars, rovers, habitats and crews are tested at places on Earth that look and feel like them. Terra Analog Atlas shows which Earth places are the closest match to each planned lunar base region and Mars site, and explains why.

Why analog sites matter

Moon

NASA named 13 candidate regions within 6° of the lunar south pole for Artemis III, chosen using Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data. Cold, ice-bearing polar terrain is best rehearsed in places like Antarctica and the Arctic.

Mars

Mars has about 38% of Earth’s gravity, an atmosphere under 1% as thick, and basaltic, dusty, salty soils. Dry, high or volcanic deserts on Earth reproduce parts of that.

Earth analogs

No Earth site copies everything. Each one matches some conditions, so the Atlas scores nine features and lets you choose what matters most.

    Datasets and where they come from

    Method

    Each place gets nine features scored from 0 to 1: coldness, day–night temperature swing, terrain roughness, dryness, basaltic rock, ice presence, low air pressure, UV or radiation exposure, and fine dust. Similarity = 100 × (1 − weighted distance) between the target and the Earth site, using your slider weights.

    Honest limits: the planetary numbers above are published NASA values. The 0 to 1 feature scores for individual sites were estimated by our team from the NASA, USGS and field-study sources listed and are not pixel-by-pixel measurements. Treat the ranking as a shortlist guide. No Earth site can reproduce low gravity, vacuum or deep-space radiation.

    Not official: this project uses public NASA data but is not made or endorsed by NASA.
